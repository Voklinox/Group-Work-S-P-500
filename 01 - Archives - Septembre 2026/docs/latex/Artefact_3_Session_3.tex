\subsection{1. Executive Summary \& Analytical
Scope}\label{executive-summary-analytical-scope}

Session 3 tests whether the means of quantitative financial and
governance metrics differ significantly across the 5 economic sectors of
the S\&P 500 index (\(N = 503\)). In accordance with course directives
(Slide 17 \& Slide 109--125), the analysis focuses on:

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Primary ANOVA}: Does \textbf{Net Profit Margin} differ
  significantly across the 5 consolidated sectors?
\item
  \textbf{Secondary ANOVA}: Does \textbf{Corporate Governance Risk}
  differ significantly across the 5 sectors?
\item
  \textbf{Parametric Assumption Checks}: Testing homogeneity of
  variances (\textbf{Levene's Test}) and normality of group residuals.
\item
  \textbf{Robust Inferential Modeling}: Executing \textbf{Welch's ANOVA}
  and non-parametric \textbf{Kruskal-Wallis} tests due to severe
  empirical variance heterogeneity.
\item
  \textbf{Post-Hoc Pairwise Comparisons}: Executing \textbf{Games-Howell
  post-hoc tests} (robust to unequal variances and group sizes) to
  pinpoint which exact industry sectors drive the differences.
\end{enumerate}

Every test adheres strictly to the \textbf{4-Step Answering Protocol}
(Slide 9).

\begin{center}\rule{0.5\linewidth}{0.5pt}\end{center}

\subsection{2. Primary Analysis: Sector Differences in Profit
Margin}\label{primary-analysis-sector-differences-in-profit-margin}

\subsubsection{2.1 Protocol Step 1 ---
IDENTIFY}\label{protocol-step-1-identify}

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Variables}:

  \begin{itemize}
  \tightlist
  \item
    Independent Variable (\(X\)): \texttt{Sector} (Nominal grouping
    factor, exactly 5 categories: \emph{Consumer, Finance, Healthcare,
    Industrials \& Energy, Tech \& Comms}).
  \item
    Dependent Variable (\(Y\)): \texttt{Profit\_Margin} (Quantitative
    continuous ratio variable, FY2025 net profit margin).
  \end{itemize}
\item
  \textbf{Test Selection}: \textbf{One-Way ANOVA}, followed by
  \textbf{Levene's Test of Homogeneity of Variances}, \textbf{Welch's
  Robust ANOVA}, and \textbf{Games-Howell Post-Hoc Pairwise
  Comparisons}.
\item
  \textbf{Methodological Justification (Slide 17 \& 118)}: When
  comparing a continuous outcome across 3 or more qualitative groups,
  multiple independent \(t\)-tests cause severe alpha inflation
  (accumulation of Type I error). One-Way ANOVA controls the family-wise
  error rate. Because financial margins exhibit heterogeneous sector
  variances, standard Fisher ANOVA is invalid; Welch's ANOVA and
  Games-Howell adjustments are statistically required.
\end{itemize}

\begin{center}\rule{0.5\linewidth}{0.5pt}\end{center}

\subsubsection{2.2 Protocol Step 2 ---
HYPOTHESISE}\label{protocol-step-2-hypothesise}

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Null Hypothesis (\(H_0\))}: There is no significant difference
  in mean profit margin across the 5 economic sectors
  (\(\mu_{\text{Consumer}} = \mu_{\text{Finance}} = \mu_{\text{Healthcare}} = \mu_{\text{Industrials}} = \mu_{\text{Tech}}\)).
  Industry sector has no bearing on average corporate profitability.
\item
  \textbf{Alternative Hypothesis (\(H_1\))}: At least one sector's
  population mean profit margin differs significantly from the others.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{1. Checking ANOVA Assumptions: Homogeneity of
Variances}\label{checking-anova-assumptions-homogeneity-of-variances}

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Levene's Test (Brown-Forsythe center = median)}:
  \[F(4, 498) = 5.392, \quad p < .001\]
\item
  \textbf{Evaluation}: The Levene test decisively \textbf{rejects the
  assumption of equal variances (\(p < .001\))}. Standard deviation
  ranges from a tight \(8.61\%\) in \emph{Industrials \& Energy} to a
  massive \(29.47\%\) in \emph{Tech \& Comms}. Standard Fisher's ANOVA
  is violated; \textbf{Welch's ANOVA must be reported} (Slide 118).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{2. Welch's Robust ANOVA \& Non-Parametric
Verification}\label{welchs-robust-anova-non-parametric-verification}

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Welch's ANOVA}:
  \[F_{\text{Welch}}(4, 191.21) = 9.302, \quad p < .001\]
\item
  \textbf{Standard Fisher's ANOVA (for comparison)}:
  \[F(4, 498) = 5.257, \quad p < .001, \quad \eta^2 = 0.0405, \quad \omega^2 = 0.0327\]
\item
  \textbf{Kruskal-Wallis Non-Parametric Test}:
  \[H(4) = 45.984, \quad p < .001\]
\item
  \textbf{Decision}: Decisively \textbf{reject \(H_0\) (\(p < .001\))}.
  Across all parametric, robust, and non-parametric tests, economic
  sectors display highly significant differences in operating profit
  margins.
\item
  \textbf{Effect Size}: \(\eta^2 = 0.0405\) indicates that
  \textbf{\(4.05\%\) of the total cross-sectional variance} in profit
  margins among S\&P 500 companies is directly explained by sector
  classification.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Substantive Findings:}\label{substantive-findings}

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{The Financial Premium}: The \textbf{Finance} sector generates
  a significantly higher average profit margin (\(22.43\%\)) than
  Consumer (\(13.29\%\)), Industrials \& Energy (\(13.10\%\)), and
  Healthcare (\(9.36\%\)), with differences exceeding \(9\) to \(13\)
  percentage points (\(p \le .001\)).
\item
  \textbf{Tech Dispersion}: Although Technology exhibits the
  second-highest mean (\(16.72\%\)) and median (\(16.28\%\)), its
  massive standard deviation (\(29.47\%\)) prevents its pairwise
  differences from reaching statistical significance after Games-Howell
  penalty adjustments.
\end{enumerate}

  Under \texttt{Variances}: Select
  \textbf{\texttt{Don\textquotesingle{}t\ assume\ equal\ (Welch\textquotesingle{}s)}}.

  Under \texttt{Assumption\ Checks}: Check
  \textbf{\texttt{Homogeneity\ test\ (Levene\textquotesingle{}s)}} and
  \textbf{\texttt{Normality\ test\ (Q-Q\ plot)}}.
\item
  Under \texttt{Post-Hoc\ Tests}: Select \textbf{\texttt{Games-Howell}},
  check \texttt{Mean\ difference},
  \texttt{Confidence\ intervals\ (95\%)}, and
  \texttt{Test\ significance}.
\item
  Under \texttt{Plots}: Check \texttt{Descriptives\ plot}, select
  \texttt{Box\ plot}, check \texttt{Show\ mean} and \texttt{Outliers}.
\end{enumerate}
